This week on the planet — one brief

24–30 August 2026 · useful anywhere · not a local forecast

realignment
book-one
weekly
Author

William Edgar Brissey

Published

August 30, 2026

This is a global note. It is written the same for every reader. It will not try to name your river, your fire line, or your power grid. Those jobs belong to the weather and civil-protection service where you live.

A page that pretended to brief eight billion streets would be a lie. A page that only briefed one country would be a private letter. This is the middle path: what the shared instruments showed, what that is allowed to mean, and how to attach it to your official warning without us mapping your house.

The three facts this week

1. The central Pacific, as published, is warm.
NOAA’s seasonal ONI for May–July 2026 is +1.39. Source: CPC oni.ascii.txt.
That is one ocean product. It is not a rain forecast for your town. In past winters, the same index has lined up with drier seasons in some places and wetter in others. We will not issue one seasonal instruction for Earth.

2. Fire satellites saw thousands of hotspots — and went blind in one large box.
A daily NOAA-20 pass on 30 August counted on the order of 5,000 thermal anomalies. One western-southern query box returned zero while another box returned thousands. Zero means we did not see that box, not “nothing is burning.” Use your national fire service for the ground you stand on.

3. The rain memory we keep is still on July.
The global CHIRPS monthly file we use for history had not published August by the time this was written. A missing month is late data, not a failed wet season. Do not read a hole as drought.

A fourth thing we do not have: a fresh number for the Atlantic overturning current people argue about. We will not invent one.

What this is allowed to mean

You might think Allowed reading
“The Pacific number is high, so my harvest is already decided.” No. Same ocean, different coasts, different signs in history.
“The fire count is high, so my hills are next.” No. Counts are global and one region was blind.
“August rain is missing from their file, so the rains failed.” No. The file is late.
“This board replaces my weather service.” No. Ever.
“I should move country because a dashboard exists.” No.

How to make it local without us writing 200 editions

You already have a local office. Use it in this order:

  1. Open the national or regional warning page for where you are (met service, civil protection, fire service). If they say leave, leave. If they say boil water, boil water.
  2. Ask only one extra question of this board: is the slow background odd compared with a long record? That is ONI, a 30-year month at a sample of cities, a fire count with holes marked.
  3. If you want a named-place sheet later (one town, one basin), that is a separate request with coordinates and the name of your official service. It is not this weekly file. We will not generate a custom brief for every reader as a hidden default.

Sample cities on the slow board exist so the South and the North both appear: Ubay, Jakarta, Mumbai, Nairobi, Lagos, Manaus, São Paulo, Mexico City, Paris. They are dots, not your catchment.

What a person anywhere can do this week

These are suggestions. They are not law.

  • Know which official account issues warnings where you sleep. Bookmark it.
  • Keep what people already keep in unsettled seasons: drinking water, a week of medicine, copies of papers, a way to hear an official alert without this site.
  • If you farm or manage water, compare this month to the same month in a long record when you have one — not to a city on another continent.
  • If a map we publish has an empty box over you, assume no coverage, not safety.

Do not treat this brief as a reason to relocate, stop treatment, or pour concrete for a private refuge.
